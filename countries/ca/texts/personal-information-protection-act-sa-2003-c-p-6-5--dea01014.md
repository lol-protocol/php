## Overview

Alberta's PIPA is recognized as substantially similar to PIPEDA, covering provincially regulated private-sector organizations and some nonprofits. The OIPC has binding order-making power, stronger enforcement authority than PIPEDA's OPC, which issues only recommendations.

## What This Means for Your Website

- Express consent is required for sensitive data of Alberta visitors; implied consent acceptable for non-sensitive
- The OIPC can issue binding orders, not just recommendations
- Mandatory breach notification applies for breaches posing real risk of significant harm
- Personal information must be destroyed when no longer needed
- Currently under legislative review (2024-2025), so changes may be coming

## Key Requirements

The OIPC Alberta enforces PIPA with penalties up to CAD $100,000 for organizations and CAD $10,000 for individuals. Binding order-making power gives the OIPC stronger enforcement than PIPEDA's OPC. Consumer requests must be fulfilled within 30 days.

## Data Subject Rights

Withdraw consent

On reasonable notice, withdraw or vary your consent at any time, except where that would frustrate a legal obligation between you and the organization; unless the Act lets it continue without consent, the organization must then stop or follow the varied consent, and the Act sets no response period.

Access your data45 days

Make a written request for access to your personal information in an organization's custody or control, as a copy or by examining the record, subject to limited exceptions; a reasonable fee may apply after a written estimate, and a longer extension needs the Commissioner's permission.

Learn how your data was used and disclosed45 days

Make a written request for the purposes for which an organization has used and is using your personal information, and the names of the persons to whom, and the circumstances in which, it has been and is being disclosed.

Correct your data45 days

Make a free written request to correct an error or omission in your personal information (not an opinion); the organization must correct it as soon as reasonably possible and, where reasonable, notify organizations it disclosed it to, or annotate it with the correction requested but not made.

Ask the Commissioner to review a decision

Ask the Information and Privacy Commissioner in writing to review any decision, act or failure to act of an organization on your request, within 30 days of being told of the decision or a longer period the Commissioner allows (no limit for an unanswered access request); the order is final, subject to judicial review.

Complain to the Privacy Commissioner

Deliver a written complaint to the Information and Privacy Commissioner within a reasonable time that an organization collected, used or disclosed your personal information in contravention of the Act, refused a correction without justification, charged an inappropriate fee or is otherwise not complying.

Act through a representative

Have any right under the Act exercised on your behalf by a person you authorize in writing, or by a guardian, trustee, attorney, personal directive agent or personal representative in the circumstances the Act sets out.

## Other North America Regulations

It established the opt-out consent model that most subsequent US state privacy laws adopted. Amended by the CPRA from January 1, 2023, it is enforced by the California Privacy Protection Agency and the Attorney General. CPRACalifornia, United States The CPRA is the most comprehensive US state privacy law with a dedicated enforcement agency (CPPA). Cross-context behavioral advertising via cookies constitutes sharing personal information, triggering opt-out obligations. GPC signals must be honored as valid opt-out requests. PIPEDACanada Canada's federal private-sector privacy law based on 10 fair information principles. Requires express consent for sensitive data and implied consent for less sensitive data. OPC guidance addresses cookies and online behavioral advertising. The CPPA replacement bill died January 2025; a new bill is expected. CIPACalifornia, United States CIPA is a 1967 California chapter covering wiretapping, eavesdropping, and other privacy invasions, not a website-specific law. Since 2022, courts have applied two of its sections to website chat, analytics, and ad tools, and any visitor can sue directly for at least $5,000 per violation, no revenue threshold. TDPSATexas, United States The TDPSA is the broadest US state privacy law: no revenue thresholds and no minimum consumer data volume thresholds. Applies to any non-small-business processing personal data of Texas residents. Must honor GPC signals since January 2025. This breadth means far more businesses are captured than under any other state law. CPAColorado, United States Colorado's CPA features the highest per-violation penalties among US state privacy laws at $20,000. Must honor GPC signals since July 2024. Participated in a joint GPC enforcement sweep with California and Connecticut in September 2025. The cure period was eliminated in January 2025.
