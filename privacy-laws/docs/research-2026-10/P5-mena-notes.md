### QA Qatar - Personal Data Privacy Protection Law (PDPPL), Law No. 13 of 2016 - confidence: medium
- enactment_date 2016-11-03: Clyde & Co and Mondaq both say the law was issued on 3 Nov 2016. Gulf Times reported the Emir issued it "on Thursday", and 3 Nov 2016 was a Thursday. The Peninsula report is dated 4 Nov 2016.
- effective_date 2016-12-29: Mondaq/vLex say it was gazetted and became law on 29 Dec 2016. A search result drawn from the Al Meezan portal says it appeared in Official Gazette issue 15 on 29 Dec 2016. Gulf Times and Simmons & Simmons both said it takes effect on gazette publication. Doubt: the Clym tracker says gazetted 3 Feb 2017 with a compliance deadline of 3 Aug 2017, and one guide says it took effect 30 days after publication. I treated both as outliers. The 6-month compliance period was extended to 29 Jan 2018 (Mondaq).
- Authority: the National Cyber Governance and Assurance Affairs (NCGAA) within the National Cyber Security Agency (NCSA) now administers the law, with the National Data Privacy Office (NDPO) issuing guidance. The Ministry of Transport and Communications (MoTC) used to do this.
- Penalties: up to QAR 1M for security, breach and marketing violations, and up to QAR 5M for special-nature and children's data (Qatar Living, Peninsula, Baker McKenzie).
- Exemptions come from the Peninsula report (personal/family use, official statistics). The QFC has its own regime (QFC Data Protection Regulations 2021).
- Retention: no statutory period was confirmed, so "Not specified".
- URL: the NCSA/NDPO site did not appear in search results, so I used the DLA Piper page.

### BH Bahrain - Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL), Law No. 30 of 2018 - confidence: high on dates, medium on authority and penalties
- enactment_date 2018-07-12: the law text (Ministry of Legal Affairs) is dated "12/07/2018 A.D." (28/10/1439 AH). DLA Piper also says 12 July 2018. Clyde & Co and Oxford Business Group put gazette publication on 19 Jul 2018, which is why BTM's "19 July" differs.
- effective_date 2019-08-01: Akin Gump, Tamimi, Oxford Business Group and Lockton agree. The law itself says it takes effect "after one year starting from the first day of the month following publication".
- Authority: the law creates a Personal Data Protection Authority. A 2019 decree (reported as Decree 78/2019, Mondaq/Cyrilla; one source gives a different number or year) gives its functions to the Ministry of Justice, Islamic Affairs and Waqf on an interim basis. Sources conflict on whether an independent board has since been set up. Signzy names MOTC, which I treated as wrong.
- Penalties: a criminal fine of BHD 1,000 to 20,000, up to 1 year imprisonment, or both (Lockton, Trowers, Clyde & Co). The administrative scale also reaches BHD 20,000, plus daily fines. Whether companies face doubled fines is disputed.
- Exemptions and retention: not confirmed, so "Not specified".
- URL: the official Ministry of Legal Affairs/LLOC English consolidated copy, which is a .docx download. If the loader needs a web page, swap in https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?c=BH&t=law (this also appeared in results).

### OM Oman - Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL), Royal Decree 6/2022 - confidence: high on enactment, medium on effective date
- enactment_date 2022-02-09: the decree was issued on 9 Feb 2022 and gazetted on 13 Feb 2022 (Official Gazette 1429). Clyde & Co, Kennedys and the decree.om text agree.
- effective_date 2023-02-13: Tamimi, Clyde & Co, BSA and PwC say it entered into force on 13 Feb 2023. Doubt: the decree's Article IV says "one year from issuance", which would be 9 Feb 2023, and IAPP gives 9 Feb 2023. I used the majority date.
- Executive Regulation (Ministerial Decision 34/2024) took effect on 5 Feb 2024, with a one-year grace period. A later amendment (MD 6/2025, a weaker source) lengthened it, and CMS says full enforcement began 5 Feb 2026. Not put in the CSV.
- Penalties: courts can fine OMR 500 to OMR 500,000 (the top figure is for unlawful cross-border transfer, Art. 29). MTCIT can fine up to OMR 2,000 administratively. There is no imprisonment under the PDPL itself (CMS, PwC, Access Partnership).
- Exemptions: not confirmed, so "Not specified". Retention: "Not specified".
- URL: the regulator's page did not appear, so I used Clyde & Co. decree.om/?p=8554 appeared and seems to host the decree text, but I could not confirm which page it is.

### JO Jordan - Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL), Law No. 24 of 2023 - confidence: medium on dates, low on penalties
- enactment_date 2023-09-17: this is the Official Gazette publication date, which Securiti, Clyde & Co and DataGuidance all give. I could not find the royal assent date, so the true enactment date may be a few days earlier.
- effective_date 2024-03-17: Clyde & Co says 17 Mar 2024, six months after publication. DataGuidance says "six months". Existing processors had a one-year grace period that ended 16 or 17 Mar 2025 (sources differ by a day).
- Authority: a Personal Data Protection Council, backed by a Unit/Directorate in the Ministry of Digital Economy and Entrepreneurship (MoDEE). The Cabinet approved a draft regulation on the Unit in 2025. The Directorate's actual operational status is unclear.
- Penalties: the most common figure is JOD 1,000 to 10,000 per violation, doubled for repeats, with a warning first (DLA Piper Art. 21, ConsentStack, Jordan News). Conflicts:
  - one tracker says JOD 5,000 to 100,000;
  - daily fines are reported with a cap of 3% or 5% of annual income;
  - a vendor guide claims JOD 1M.
  I used "Up to JOD 10k per violation (doubled if repeated)".
- key_requirements: the search snippets confirmed only the warning-first enforcement, Council licences/permits and retroactive coverage. "Consent, data subject rights, controller and processor duties" is general, not snippet-confirmed. data_categories is "Personal data" only, because I did not confirm a sensitive-data tier. Exemptions and retention are "Not specified".
- The modee.gov.jo "Personal Data Protection Directorate" page also appeared, but its URL (.../Pages/False) looked unreliable, so I used Clyde & Co.

### MA Morocco - Law No. 09-08 (Law 09-08) - confidence: high on enactment, LOW on effective date
- enactment_date 2009-02-18: Dahir No. 1-09-15 of 22 Safar 1430 (18 Feb 2009), published in Bulletin Officiel No. 5714 on 5 Mar 2009. CMS, DPO Consulting and the DGSSI-hosted copy agree.
- effective_date 2009-03-05: this is the gazette date and is an INFERENCE. No search result stated an entry-into-force date, and Morocco's default is effect on publication. Drop or fix this field if you want strictness. The implementing Decree 2-09-165 is reported as 21 May 2009 (one source). Legal500 mislabels Dahir 1-09-15 as the decree.
- Authority: the CNDP, a 7-member body appointed by the King, and the only regulator.
- Penalties: the law has fines and imprisonment, but no amount appeared in results, so "Not specified".
- Amendments: one site claims a 2020 amendment ("Decree 2-20-03"), but other sources found no amending instrument, so I ignored it. A reform of the law has been discussed.
- Exemptions come from one secondary source (DPO Consulting) and should be checked against the official text.
- The URL is a CMS French page about Law 09-08. The CNDP site and DGSSI copy did not appear as URLs.
